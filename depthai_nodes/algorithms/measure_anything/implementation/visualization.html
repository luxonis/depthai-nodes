01 / What you need

Depth gives the shape.
Images check the boundaries.

The algorithm combines 3D geometry with three synchronized camera views. It measures one scene at a time; it does not track parcels or smooth measurements over time.

1 · A 3D scene

A point cloud in millimeters

Each depth point describes a surface in 3D. The algorithm groups points above the support surface into possible parcels, then measures their supported outer edges.

Depth generation is upstream. This geometric parcel algorithm does not need a parcel-detection neural network.

2 · A known surface

The plane under the parcels

A supplied support plane defines zero height and the direction “up.” The camera and the plane must use the same calibrated coordinate frame.

A wrong plane can affect both which points count as foreground and the reported height.

3 · Calibrated views

Color, left and right images

Image edges and depth boundaries help determine whether a parcel is fully visible. At least one view must support the complete, unobstructed outer silhouette.

The image pixels, lens calibration and point cloud must correspond to the same scene.

The evaluated camera profile

Unrectified color at 2000 × 1500 and left/right mono at 1280 × 800. Other resolutions or rectification policies have not been established as equivalent: some visibility thresholds are measured in image pixels.

Integration details

Color is decoded to RGB uint8; stereo views use grayscale uint8. Scene points are finite float32 XYZ in millimeters, with a supplied upward plane and per-view intrinsics, distortion and scene-to-camera transforms. Source images and point clouds must be synchronized. The OAK application used NeuralDepth LARGE at 768 × 480 upstream; that depth grid is distinct from the three source-image grids. The current parcel wrapper warns on a different source-image size and continues processing without resizing or retuning.

02 / How a measurement is made

Find it. Check it. Measure it.

Step through the simulated scene to understand the workflow. Then compare it with the two real captures below.

Separate points above the surface, reduce them into small 3D cells, and connect neighboring cells into parcel proposals.

Use the camera views to reject clipped or obstructed proposals. A complete silhouette in one view is enough to proceed.

Measure

Remove isolated returns, fit and refine a footprint, then estimate a supported height above the plane.

Simulated explanation · not a scored capture
Visibility is an eligibility check, not a measurement guarantee. A parcel can be fully visible while noisy, incomplete or irregular surface returns still produce a dimension error. A rejected proposal is not sent to the measurement stage. A successful scene may contain no eligible parcels.
How computation is scheduled

The frozen v002 configuration prepares the three camera projections with three workers, then checks candidates sequentially. This reduces CPU contention. Contiguous depth-probe coordinates avoid an unnecessary intermediate tensor. The application controller also keeps the newest pending scene ready while one measurement is in flight. The speed section explains the cost of scene processing, fitting and the application pipeline.

find_candidates: sample → cluster → visibility checks → eligible point sets
measure: remove isolated points → footprint → supported height → cuboid

Detection uses 18 mm voxels and 26 mm connectivity. Measurement uses an 8 mm voxel grid, local support within 24 mm, and independent footprint and height support rules. The full evaluated parameters are in the evidence section.

05 / Accuracy in the evaluated data

Every object found.
Dimension error still matters.

Validation was used during development. The approved held-out test evaluated the frozen candidate separately. These small sets describe the measured scenes, not a guarantee for every parcel, camera placement or environment.

“Within ±5 mm”

The length, width and height must each be no more than 5 mm from the reference. A parcel can miss this test even when two dimensions are excellent.

Mean absolute error, or MAE

The average size of the error in one dimension, ignoring direction. MAE is not a worst-case bound; the width outlier above is much larger than the average.

Positive and negative error

Signed error is measured minus reference. Positive values are too large; negative values are too small. The green band in the error plots is the ±5 mm tolerance.

One scene is shared work.
Each parcel adds a fit.

Detection and visibility operate on the scene; dimensions are then fitted for each eligible parcel. The measurements below describe this algorithm on OAK-4.

Inside the algorithm timer

Scene proposal generation and visibility → eligible-point extraction → all parcel fits → adapter checks and completion of every worker. Inputs are already decoded. Each scene has one warm-up before three timed runs; component construction and warm-up are excluded.

Why count alone is insufficient

The scene must be projected and clustered before any parcel is measured. The number of proposals, retained points, visibility work and parcel geometry affect cost. More parcels can share that scene work; a lower amortized cost does not mean the second parcel is free.

07 / Practical limits

Evaluate the scene you intend to measure.

The best and worst cases are useful examples, not a complete map of failure modes.

Keep the outer shape visible

Clipped or obstructed silhouettes are rejected or marked uncertain. Touching parcels can form one connected proposal; very sparse objects can fail the minimum support checks.

Do not treat a fit as certainty

Supported surface points can still produce an oversized footprint. The difficult case passes eligibility but misses the width tolerance by a wide margin.

Check calibration and your population

Image geometry, the support plane and source synchronization are part of the measurement. Test representative parcel shapes, surfaces, spacing and camera placements under your target tolerance.
